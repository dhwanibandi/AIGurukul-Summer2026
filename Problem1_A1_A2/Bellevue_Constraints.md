# City of Bellevue Site Development and Zoning Constraints

### 1. Basic Plot and Surface Scope
* **Lot Dimensions:** The overall build space is defined by the absolute length of the property lines, the width of the property lines, and the total square footage of the lot.
* **Front Yard Landscaping Rule:** Paved surfaces like driveways or walkways cannot dominate the front yard. At least **50 percent** of the total front yard setback area must be kept as open, unpaved green space.
* **Floor Area Ratio Limitation:** The combined interior square footage of all floors inside the home is capped at **50 percent** of the total lot area. If the design exceeds this 50 percent threshold, the left and right side setbacks must automatically be increased to a minimum of **7.5 feet** per side.
* **Maximum Structural Height:** The vertical peak of the main house structure is restricted to **30 feet** above the average ground grade line.

---

### 2. Specific Yard Setbacks by Zoning District
A yard setback defines the minimum distance a building wall must stay away from the property lines. Bellevue establishes these absolute minimum boundaries based on the specific residential zone:

* **Suburban Estate District (R-1):**  
  * The front wall must sit at least **35 feet** back from the front property line.
  * The rear wall must sit at least **25 feet** forward from the back property line.
  * The left and right side walls must sit at least **5 feet** away from the side lines, and the combined total width of both side yards added together must equal at least **20 feet**.
* **Low Density Residential District (R-1.8):**  
  * The front wall must sit at least **30 feet** back from the front property line.
  * The rear wall must sit at least **25 feet** forward from the back property line.
  * The left and right side walls must sit at least **5 feet** away from the side lines, and the combined total width of both side yards added together must equal at least **15 feet**.
* **Moderate Density Residential Districts (R-2.5 and R-3.5):**  
  * The front wall must sit at least **20 feet** back from the front property line.
  * The rear wall must sit at least **25 feet** forward from the back property line.
  * The left and right side walls must sit at least **5 feet** away from the side lines, and the combined total width of both side yards added together must equal at least **15 feet**.
* **Medium Density Residential District (R-4 and R-5):**  
  * The front wall must sit at least **20 feet** back from the front property line.
  * The rear wall must sit at least **20 feet** forward from the back property line.
  * The left and right side walls must sit at least **5 feet** away from the side lines, and the combined total width of both side yards added together must equal at least **15 feet**.
* **High-Density Single-Family District (R-7.5):**  
  * The front wall must sit at least **20 feet** back from the front property line.
  * The rear wall must sit at least **20 feet** forward from the back property line.
  * The left and right side walls must sit at least **5 feet** away from the side lines, and the combined total width of both side yards added together must equal at least **10 feet**.

---

### 3. Lot Coverage Footprint Limits
Lot coverage restricts the absolute ground space covered by structures. This cap changes depending on the neighborhood density:

* **Low and Moderate Density Zones (R-1, R-1.8, R-2.5, R-3.5, and R-4):** The total ground footprint of the house and all accessory buildings combined cannot cover more than **35 percent** of the total lot area.
* **Medium and High Density Zones (R-5 and R-7.5):** The total ground footprint of all structures combined is allowed to expand up to a maximum of **40 percent** of the total lot area.

---

### 4. Architectural Projections and Encroachment Tolerances
* **Minor Appendages:** Small structural features like chimneys, bay windows, and roof eaves are permitted to extend past the main house walls and project up to **2 feet** into any required yard setback.
* **Property Line Clearance:** Regardless of any allowed projection, no building element or architectural feature is ever permitted to come closer than **3 feet** to any property boundary line.

---

### 5. Parking, Garage, and Access Alignment
* **Garage Door Setback:** To prevent parked vehicles from sticking out and blocking public sidewalks, any part of the building wall containing a primary garage door vehicle entrance must maintain a strict minimum setback of **20 feet** from the front property line.
* **Access Co-Location Safety:** The primary pedestrian path to the main front door and the vehicular driveway must both face, connect to, and draw access from the exact same front street frontage.

---

### 6. Tree Preservation and Canopy Keep-Out Zones
* **Significant Tree Classification:** Any healthy tree measuring **6 inches** or wider in trunk diameter (measured at standard chest height) is classified as protected.
* **Canopy Retention Mandate:** Any new site layout must actively retain and preserve at least **30 percent** of the total combined diameter inches of all protected trees currently living on the plot.
* **Critical Protection Area:** The layout must establish a strict circular exclusion zone around the center point of each protected tree. The radius of this circle is calculated by allocating **1 foot** of clear radius for every **1 inch** of tree trunk diameter. No structural foundation line or corner vertex of the house layout may cross inside this boundary.
