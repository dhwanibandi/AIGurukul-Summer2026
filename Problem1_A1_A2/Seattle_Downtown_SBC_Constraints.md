1. The Global Lot Boundary Matrix
Establish site constraints based on the legal property boundaries.

The Length Variable: 
The length of the parcel stretching from the front street property line to the back boundary line.

The Width Variable: 
The width of the parcel stretching between the left and right property lines.

The Absolute Ground Area: 
The total square footage of the lot calculated by multiplying the lot length by the lot width.

2. Specific Yard Setback Classifications
A yard setback represents a strict "no-build" buffer zone measured inward from the property borders. The house periphery is entirely restricted from intersecting these zones.

Neighborhood Residential Zones (NR1, NR2, NR3 - Detached Homes & Townhomes)
Front Setback Rule: 
The house wall must sit a minimum of 15 feet back from the front property line. However, if neighbor houses on either side are further back, it matches their average up to a maximum constraint of 20 feet.

Rear Setback Rule: 
The back wall of the house must sit a minimum of 15 feet forward from the back property line.

Side Setback Rule:
The left and right side walls must sit a minimum of 5 feet away from their respective side property lines.

Lowrise 1 (LR1) and Lowrise 2 (LR2) Multifamily Zones (Rowhouses & Small Apartments)
Front Setback Rule: The front wall must maintain an average distance of 7 feet from the front line, and no single point along the front wall can be closer than 5 feet.

Rear Setback Rule: 
The back wall must maintain a minimum distance of 7 feet from the rear line. If the rear of the lot directly borders a public alleyway, this setback drops to 0 feet, allowing the structure to be built right against the alley line.

Side Setback Rule: 
The left and right side walls must maintain a minimum distance of 5 feet from the side lines.

Lowrise 3 (LR3) Multifamily Zones (High-Density Multi-Story Buildings)
Front Setback Rule: The front wall must maintain an average distance of 7 feet and a hard minimum of 5 feet from the front line.

Rear Setback Rule: 
If there is no rear alleyway, the back wall must sit a minimum of 10 feet forward from the back property line. If an alley is present, the setback drops to 0 feet.

Side Setback Rule: 
If the side wall of the structure is 40 feet long or shorter, it must stay 5 feet away from the side line. If the side wall is longer than 40 feet, it must maintain an average distance of 7 feet and a minimum distance of 5 feet from the side line.

3. Lot Coverage Limit Classifications 
Lot coverage rules restrict the total combined horizontal surface area of all roofs, structures, and buildings on a plot that sit 36 inches or higher above the ground. This limit prevents overbuilding, protects natural earth for urban stormwater drainage, and ensures green breathing room between neighboring properties.

Neighborhood Residential Standard Lots: For any standard house lot measuring 5,000 square feet or larger, the maximum ground footprint of all structures combined cannot exceed 35 percent of the total lot area.

Neighborhood Residential Under-Sized Lots: For small lots measuring less than 5,000 square feet, the maximum footprint cannot exceed 15 percent of the lot area plus an added flat allowance of 1,000 square feet.

Neighborhood Residential Stacked Flat Revisions: For new multi-unit configurations built as stacked flat structures, the maximum coverage boundary expands up to 60 percent of the total lot area.

Lowrise 1 (LR1) and Lowrise 2 (LR2) Townhouses: The maximum ground coverage of all structural footprints cannot exceed 40 percent of the total lot area.

Lowrise 2 (LR2) Stacked Apartments: The maximum footprint limit increases to 50 percent of the total lot area.

Lowrise 3 (LR3) Zones: The maximum footprint limit is capped between 45 percent and 50 percent of the total lot area, depending on the exact housing typology handled.

4. Tree Protection Keep-Out Zones
Seattle treats mature urban trees as immovable structural obstructions. Harming or removing them carries significant civil penalties and freezing of building permits.

Protected and Exceptional Trees: Any tree with a trunk diameter of 6 inches or wider measured at a standard chest height of 4.5 feet is protected. Any tree with a trunk diameter of 30 inches or wider is classified as an "Exceptional Tree" and cannot be cut down under any circumstances.

The Basic Tree Protection Radius: The system must draw a strict circular exclusion ring around the exact center coordinate of the tree. The radius of this circle is calculated as 1 foot of clearance for every 1 inch of trunk diameter. (For example, a tree with a 30-inch trunk diameter dictates a strict 30-foot circular radius keep-out zone). No corner or wall line of the house periphery polygon can cross inside this circular boundary.

5. Garage, Driveway, and Parking Infrastructure Rules
The Garage Sidewalk Safety Setback: Even if the living spaces of a house are legally allowed to sit 15 feet from the front street line, any part of the structure containing a vehicle garage door entrance must be set back a minimum of 20 feet from the front property line. This prevents parked vehicles from overhanging and blocking public pedestrian walkways.

Driveway Width Limits: The driveway path where it crosses the front property boundary line must be a minimum of 10 feet wide and cannot exceed a maximum width of 20 feet.

6. Fire Safety Access and Entrance Co-Location
Single Frontage Access Egress: To ensure direct visibility for emergency response teams and fire hoses, the primary main entrance door of the home and the vehicle driveway/parking access must interface with and feed out to the exact same public street frontage side of the plot.

Fire Wall Separations: If a garage structure is fully attached to the house periphery, it must share a continuous fire-rated common boundary wall with the living spaces. If the garage is designed as a detached structure, it must maintain a clean 5-foot open spatial separation from the main house periphery to avoid complex firewall engineering codes.

7. Permitted Architectural Encroachments (Setback Overlap Exceptions)
Your validation logic should not fail the entire layout if small architectural details push past the primary setback lines, provided they match these specific allowances:

Fireplaces, Chimneys, and Bay Windows: Minor external appendages like masonry fireplaces or bay windows are allowed to extend a maximum of 2 feet into any required yard setback, but they must remain a minimum of 3 feet completely clear of the actual legal property line.

Unenclosed Porches and Ground Decks: Covered porches, entry steps, and backyard decks are allowed to push 5 feet into a front yard setback or 3 feet into a side yard setback, provided the surface platform sits less than 4 feet high off the natural ground grade.

Mechanical Heat Pumps and EV Chargers: External machinery like HVAC heat pumps or electric vehicle charging pillars can be placed inside yard setbacks, provided they maintain a strict 3-foot clearance buffer from the neighboring property line.

8. Structural Height and Bulk Caps
Maximum Residential Base Height: The vertical height of a standard home structure is capped at a maximum of 30 feet (or up to 32 feet depending on specific roof pitches) above the average calculated ground grade line.

Lowrise Height Extrusions: Structural heights in Lowrise zones scale upward from 30 feet in LR1, up to 40 or 50 feet in high-density LR3 apartment zones.

Rooftop Feature Exceptions: Vertical elements like chimneys, plumbing vents, and roof ridges are legally permitted to extend an additional 4 feet above the standard maximum height caps.

Floor Area Ratio (FAR Density Cap): The absolute combined interior square footage of all floors inside the house cannot exceed a density ratio of 0.5 to 0.6 times the total square footage of the ground lot.
